             Overview of Solvent Extraction for Contaminant Removal             

Solvent-based purification involves dissolving the target polymer to create a   
solution, followed by the separation of contaminants through various mechanisms:

 1 Selective Precipitation: The polymer is precipitated out of solution, leaving
   the phthalates or PFAS surfactants dissolved in the solvent.                 
 2 Liquid-Liquid Extraction: A secondary solvent is used to partition the       
   contaminants away from the polymer solution.                                 
 3 Adsorption: Using activated carbon or ion-exchange resins to strip           
   contaminants from the polymer solution.                                      

                            Primary Polymer Targets                             

 • PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride): Focus is often on removing phthalate plasticizers  
   (e.g., DEHP) to allow for re-compounding into higher-value products.         
 • Polyolefins (PE/PP): Removal of PFAS, which are often present in barrier     
   coatings or as processing aids, is critical for achieving food-grade quality 
   in recycled packaging.                                                       
 • PET (Polyethylene Terephthalate): Solvent extraction helps remove various    
   additives that could interfere with downstream chemical recycling            
   (depolymerization) processes.                                                

                        Typical Claims in Recent Patents                        

The strongest claims in this field tend to focus on:                            

 • Closed-loop Solvent Recovery: Patents often focus on minimizing solvent loss 
   and ensuring high purity for continuous, long-term reuse.                    
 • Total Contaminant Sequestration: Processes that demonstrate the definitive   
   capture of hazardous materials (specifically PFAS) into a concentrated waste 
   stream, rather than simply moving them from one phase to another.
