## Comprehensive Safety Audit (post-hoc, all solvents)

The agent's safety-analyst only highlighted the worst offenders per scheme. A full GSK G-score lookup for every solvent reveals that Scheme 1 ("safest") is still predominantly Problematic-rated. GSK G-score: 0-10 scale, geometric mean of Environmental/Health/Safety/Waste. Thresholds: >=8 Excellent, >=6 Good, >=4 Problematic, <4 Hazardous.

### Safety subagent failure modes

1. **Hallucinated G-scores**: Nitrobenzene "G-score 1" is fabricated -- solvent is not in the GSK dataset. The agent invented a plausible-sounding score rather than reporting "not found".
2. **Incomplete coverage**: 3 of ~15 unique solvents (m-Cresol, Nitrobenzene, Phenol) are missing from the GSK dataset. The safety-analyst did not flag this gap or attempt alternative lookups (e.g., PubChem tools).
3. **Relative vs absolute framing**: Calling Scheme 1 "safest" without noting that 5/7 solvents are still "Problematic" is misleading. The agent should contextualize rankings against absolute thresholds.
4. **No comparison to paper**: The safety-analyst did not compare against known safer alternatives (1,2-PDO, GVL) that achieve the same separations.

## Key Finding: Cost-Safety Trade-off

The agent identified a fundamental trade-off: **the cheapest process uses the most dangerous solvents**. Scheme 3 achieves low operating costs by using aggressive solvents (DCM, TFA, Phenol) that dissolve polymers at ambient temperature, eliminating heating costs but introducing severe safety and environmental risks. Scheme 1 represents the best balance: moderate costs with fewer extreme hazards.

## Comparison with Paper (Sanchez-Rivera et al. 2025)

The paper uses: Toluene (PS), THF (PVC), o-Xylene (LDPE/HDPE/PP), DMSO/Water (EVOH), 1,2-PDO (PA66/6), GVL (PET), DMSO (PA6).

- **Scheme 1** is closest to the paper: uses xylene for polyolefins (paper uses o-xylene), THF for PVC, DMSO for EVOH
- The paper avoids chlorinated solvents (DCM), fluorinated acids (TFA), and nitrobenzene entirely
- The paper's solvent choices reflect a balanced approach similar to Scheme 1, prioritizing practical safety over minimum cost

## Observations

1. **Ordering changed again**: All 3 schemes now dissolve PS first (like the paper), not EVOH first (as in Trace 4). This inconsistency across runs suggests the agent's ordering is not deterministic.
2. **New solvents introduced**: Decalin, Dichloromethane, TFA, Nitrobenzene, MEK, Phenol -- none of which appeared in Traces 3 or 4. The expanded tool budget let the agent explore more of the solvent space.
3. **Safety grounded in tools**: The safety-analyst made 14 `get_solvent_gscore` calls, so safety rankings are data-backed (unlike Trace 2 where safety used 0 tools).
4. **Token efficiency**: 628K total tokens vs 1,573K for Trace 4 (single agent, 3 schemes only). The sequential 3-agent approach was 2.5x more token-efficient despite doing more work.
5. **DuckDB race condition**: One `get_solvent_gscore` call failed with "result closed" error -- a concurrency issue in the async DuckDB wrapper that should be investigated.
