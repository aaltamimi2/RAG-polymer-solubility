## Summary
Dichloroacetonitrile (DCAN) is a highly toxic, unregulated nitrogenous disinfection byproduct formed during water chlorination, with genotoxicity and cytotoxicity exceeding regulated DBPs like haloacetic acids. While not commercially produced as a solvent, it is available as a research chemical through custom synthesis at high cost (~$3,500/kg).

## Additional Notes
- **Toxicology**: LC₅₀ 5.73×10⁻⁵ M (CHO cells), one order of magnitude more toxic than bromate (9.63×10⁻⁴ M)
- **Genotoxicity**: Direct-acting mutagen; induces DNA strand breaks in cultured human lymphoblastic cells and sister chromatid exchanges (SCE)
- **Carcinogenicity**: IARC Group 3 (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity to humans)
- **Acute toxicity**: Oral LD₅₀ = 270 mg/kg (male mice)
- **Target organs**: Hepatic and renal damage observed (increased ALT, ALP, BUN, retinol-binding protein in rats at 44 mg/kg)
- **Environmental fate**: Rapidly hydrolyzes in water; biodegradation not expected to be important; estimated BCF = 3 (low bioconcentration in fish)
- **Formation pathways**: Produced during chlorination of water containing amino acids (aspartic acid, tyrosine, tryptophan), kynurenine, kynurenic acid, and other nitrogen-containing organics
- **DBP mitigation**: UV treatment reduces DCAN formation by 67.95% compared to chlorination alone
- **Wildfire impact**: DCAN formation stimulated at moderate heating temperatures during wildfire events
- **Applications**: Research chemical for synthesis of chiral α,α-dichloro-β-aminonitriles and α,α-dialkyl-substituted nitriles; not used as industrial solvent
- **Regulatory**: Unregulated DBP with high toxicity; controlled substance requiring documentation
